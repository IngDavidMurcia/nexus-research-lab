# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the official methodological standards adopted by the NEXUS Research Lab.

These standards define the principles governing the planning, execution, validation and documentation of scientific research regardless of discipline, implementation platform or publication target.

Every research project SHALL align with these standards unless explicitly authorized otherwise by the NEXUS Director.

---

# 2. Scope

This standard applies to every research activity performed within the NEXUS Research Lab, including:

- exploratory research;
- experimental research;
- computational research;
- engineering projects;
- software research;
- systematic reviews;
- literature reviews;
- proof-of-concept studies;
- multidisciplinary investigations.

These principles SHALL apply independently of scientific domain.

---

# 3. Methodological Principles

Every research project SHALL comply with the following methodological principles:

- scientific rigor;
- objectivity;
- reproducibility;
- traceability;
- transparency;
- logical consistency;
- evidence-based reasoning;
- documentation completeness;
- ethical responsibility;
- continuous verification.

Methodological quality SHALL always take precedence over execution speed.

---

# 5. Research Design Standards

Research design SHALL begin with a clearly defined scientific objective.

Every research design SHOULD identify:

- research problem;
- research objectives;
- hypotheses or research questions;
- scope;
- assumptions;
- constraints;
- success criteria;
- expected deliverables.

Research design SHALL remain internally consistent throughout the project.

---

## 5.1 Research Objectives

Research objectives SHALL:

- be measurable whenever applicable;
- remain aligned with the research problem;
- define expected outcomes;
- support objective evaluation.

Objectives SHOULD avoid ambiguity.

---

## 5.2 Research Questions

Research questions SHOULD:

- address identifiable knowledge gaps;
- be scientifically answerable;
- support evidence collection;
- remain consistent with project objectives.

Research questions SHALL guide methodological decisions throughout the project.

---

# 6. Evidence Standards

Every scientific conclusion SHALL be supported by appropriate evidence.

Evidence MAY include:

- experimental observations;
- empirical measurements;
- computational results;
- validated datasets;
- peer-reviewed literature;
- systematic reviews;
- official standards;
- reproducible simulations.

Evidence SHALL remain traceable throughout the research lifecycle.

---

## 6.1 Evidence Quality

Evidence SHOULD satisfy the following attributes:

- relevance;
- reliability;
- verifiability;
- completeness;
- reproducibility;
- scientific credibility.

Evidence SHALL be evaluated according to its methodological suitability rather than quantity alone.

---

## 6.2 Evidence Traceability

Every significant research finding SHALL be traceable to one or more identifiable evidence sources.

Traceability SHALL include:

- origin;
- acquisition method;
- processing history;
- validation status;
- associated documentation.

Missing evidence SHALL be considered a methodological deficiency.

---

# 7. Validation Standards

Every research project SHALL include an explicit validation strategy.

Validation MAY include:

- experimental validation;
- statistical validation;
- computational validation;
- analytical validation;
- comparative evaluation;
- expert review;
- benchmark comparison;
- replication studies.

Validation methods SHALL be appropriate to the research objectives.

---

## 7.1 Reproducibility

Research SHOULD be reproducible whenever technically and ethically feasible.

Projects SHOULD preserve:

- methodological descriptions;
- datasets or dataset references;
- software versions;
- configuration parameters;
- experimental conditions;
- execution environment.

Reproducibility limitations SHALL be explicitly documented.

---

## 7.2 Quality Assurance

Methodological quality assurance SHALL include:

- internal consistency verification;
- evidence verification;
- documentation review;
- methodological review;
- governance verification.

Quality assurance SHALL occur throughout the project lifecycle rather than only at project completion.

---

# 8. Documentation Standards

Every research activity SHALL generate sufficient documentation to support future review, replication and auditing.

Documentation SHOULD include:

- research objectives;
- methodological decisions;
- implementation records;
- experimental logs;
- analysis reports;
- validation results;
- revision history;
- final conclusions.

Documentation SHALL remain synchronized with the Project Object.

---

## 8.1 Documentation Quality

Research documentation SHALL be:

- complete;
- structured;
- understandable;
- traceable;
- version controlled;
- internally consistent.

Incomplete documentation SHALL reduce methodological confidence.

---

## 8.2 Knowledge Preservation

Knowledge generated during research SHALL be preserved whenever possible.

Knowledge preservation MAY include:

- reusable methodologies;
- reusable datasets;
- reusable workflows;
- reusable software components;
- lessons learned;
- best practices.

Institutional knowledge SHALL remain available for future projects.

---

# 9. Compliance

A research project SHALL be considered methodologically compliant if it:

- follows the defined research lifecycle;
- satisfies methodological principles;
- maintains evidence traceability;
- applies appropriate validation;
- preserves reproducibility where feasible;
- generates complete documentation;
- complies with governance requirements.

Projects failing these criteria SHOULD be returned for methodological revision.
