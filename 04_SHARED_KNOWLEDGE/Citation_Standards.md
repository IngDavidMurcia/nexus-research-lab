# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the official citation and reference management standards adopted by the NEXUS Research Lab.

These standards define how scientific sources SHALL be identified, evaluated, referenced and preserved throughout the research lifecycle.

The purpose of this standard is to ensure:

- scientific traceability;
- attribution accuracy;
- evidence reliability;
- intellectual integrity;
- reproducible research practices.

---

# 2. Scope

This standard applies to every scientific artifact generated within NEXUS, including:

- research papers;
- technical reports;
- literature reviews;
- experimental documentation;
- project reports;
- publication packages;
- reviewer reports;
- integrity audits.

Citation practices SHALL apply regardless of scientific discipline or publication venue.

---

# 3. Citation Principles

Every scientific claim requiring external support SHALL be accompanied by an appropriate reference.

Citation practices SHALL preserve:

- attribution;
- transparency;
- traceability;
- intellectual ownership;
- scientific verification.

---

## 3.1 Attribution Principle

Researchers SHALL correctly acknowledge the origin of:

- theories;
- methodologies;
- datasets;
- algorithms;
- experimental procedures;
- previously published findings.

Failure to provide appropriate attribution SHALL be considered a scientific integrity violation.

---

## 3.2 Evidence Alignment

A citation SHALL accurately support the statement it accompanies.

Researchers SHALL verify that:

- the source actually contains the claimed information;
- the interpretation matches the original context;
- the citation strength is proportional to the scientific claim.

A reference SHALL NOT be used only because it contains related keywords.

---

## 3.3 Citation Completeness

Scientific documents SHOULD include references for:

- foundational concepts;
- previous research;
- experimental methods;
- datasets;
- software tools;
- theoretical frameworks;
- statistical approaches.

Common knowledge MAY be excluded when appropriate.

---

# 4. Reference Quality Standards

References SHALL be evaluated according to scientific relevance, reliability and authority.

Preferred sources include:

- peer-reviewed journal articles;
- academic books;
- conference proceedings;
- recognized scientific databases;
- official technical standards;
- institutional publications.

---

## 4.1 Source Reliability Hierarchy

Sources SHOULD generally be prioritized according to the following hierarchy:

```
Level 1

Peer-reviewed scientific publications

↓

Level 2

Academic and institutional sources

↓

Level 3

Recognized technical documentation

↓

Level 4

Professional publications

↓

Level 5

General information sources
```

The appropriate source level SHALL depend on the research objective.

---

## 4.2 Source Selection Criteria

References SHOULD be evaluated according to:

- scientific relevance;
- publication quality;
- methodological rigor;
- publication date;
- citation impact when applicable;
- author credibility;
- institutional reliability.

A highly cited source SHALL NOT automatically replace methodological evaluation.

---

# 5. Citation Management

Every project SHOULD maintain structured reference management.

Citation records SHOULD preserve:

- author information;
- publication title;
- publication venue;
- publication date;
- identifiers;
- access information;
- citation format;
- relevance to the project.

---

## 5.1 Reference Identifiers

Whenever available, references SHOULD include persistent identifiers such as:

- DOI;
- ISBN;
- PMID;
- arXiv identifier;
- official repository identifier.

Persistent identifiers improve long-term traceability.

---

## 5.2 Citation Formats

NEXUS MAY support multiple citation styles, including:

- APA;
- IEEE;
- Vancouver;
- Chicago;
- journal-specific formats.

The selected citation style SHALL be defined according to publication requirements or project specifications.

---

# 6. Source Verification

Every reference included in a scientific artifact SHALL undergo appropriate verification prior to publication.

The level of verification SHALL be proportional to the importance of the cited source within the research.

Source verification SHALL preserve:

- authenticity;
- accessibility;
- attribution accuracy;
- scientific relevance;
- traceability.

---

## 6.1 Verification Procedures

Researchers SHOULD verify:

- author identity;
- publication venue;
- publication date;
- persistent identifiers;
- availability of the original source;
- correspondence between citation and cited content.

Verification MAY employ manual review, institutional databases or trusted bibliographic management systems.

---

## 6.2 Reference Consistency

Every citation SHALL correspond to a complete reference entry.

Reference consistency SHALL ensure:

- identical author names;
- correct publication year;
- accurate title;
- matching identifiers;
- complete bibliographic metadata.

Broken consistency SHALL be corrected before publication.

---

## 6.3 Retracted Publications

Researchers SHOULD determine whether referenced publications have been retracted, superseded or significantly corrected.

When citing a retracted publication:

- the retraction status SHALL be explicitly acknowledged when scientifically relevant;
- its use SHOULD be limited to historical or methodological discussion;
- it SHALL NOT be presented as valid scientific evidence.

---

# 7. AI-Assisted Citation Handling

Artificial Intelligence MAY assist researchers during literature exploration and citation management.

However, every AI-generated reference SHALL be independently verified before inclusion in any scientific artifact.

---

## 7.1 AI Citation Verification

Researchers SHALL verify:

- publication existence;
- bibliographic metadata;
- DOI validity;
- author information;
- publication source;
- accessibility.

AI-generated references SHALL NEVER bypass human verification.

---

## 7.2 Fabricated References

Fabricated, hallucinated or unverifiable references SHALL NOT be incorporated into any NEXUS research artifact.

If fabricated references are identified:

- they SHALL be removed;
- affected conclusions SHOULD be re-evaluated;
- the incident SHOULD be documented when appropriate;
- additional verification MAY be required.

Fabricated citations SHALL be considered a critical quality issue.

---

## 7.3 Citation Traceability

Every citation SHOULD remain traceable throughout the complete research lifecycle.

Traceability MAY include:

- reference databases;
- persistent identifiers;
- institutional repositories;
- version-controlled bibliographies;
- research knowledge graphs.

Long-term traceability SHALL be preferred whenever feasible.

---

# 8. Compliance

A scientific artifact SHALL be considered compliant with this standard if it:

- applies appropriate citation practices;
- preserves attribution accuracy;
- maintains evidence traceability;
- employs reliable scientific sources;
- verifies AI-generated references;
- documents bibliographic information consistently;
- complies with governance requirements.

Non-compliant citation practices SHOULD be corrected before publication.

## Annex B — Preferred Scientific Sources

Researchers SHOULD prioritize references originating from:

- peer-reviewed journals;
- systematic reviews;
- meta-analyses;
- conference proceedings;
- governmental agencies;
- international standards organizations;
- academic publishers;
- institutional repositories;
- recognized scientific databases.

Lower-authority sources SHOULD only be used when scientifically justified.

---

## Annex C — Citation Lifecycle

Every scientific reference SHOULD conceptually follow the lifecycle below.

```text
Literature Search

↓

Source Selection

↓

Quality Evaluation

↓

Evidence Verification

↓

Citation Integration

↓

Reference Validation

↓

Peer Review

↓

Publication

↓

Long-Term Preservation
```

This lifecycle SHALL remain compatible with the NEXUS Workflow specification.

---

## Annex D — Bibliographic Metadata

Whenever possible, references SHOULD preserve the following metadata:

Mandatory:

- Authors
- Title
- Publication Year
- Publication Venue

Recommended:

- DOI
- ISBN
- PMID
- URL
- Publisher
- Volume
- Issue
- Pages
- Language
- Access Date (for web resources)

Persistent identifiers SHOULD always be preferred over temporary URLs.

---

## Annex E — Future Evolution

Future versions MAY include:

- automated DOI validation;
- citation knowledge graphs;
- semantic reference verification;
- duplicate citation detection;
- FAIR reference compliance;
- Crossref integration;
- ORCID integration;
- automated bibliography auditing;
- citation quality scoring.

These extensions SHALL preserve backward compatibility with this standard.

---

# Conformance Statement

This document defines the official citation and reference management standards adopted by the NEXUS Research Lab.

It establishes a technology-independent framework for attribution, evidence traceability, bibliographic integrity and reference verification across all scientific activities.

Every compliant implementation SHALL preserve these citation management characteristics regardless of discipline, publication venue or software platform.
