# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the editorial standards adopted by the NEXUS Research Lab.

These standards SHALL be shared by every specialist participating in manuscript generation, scientific review, publication preparation and integrity verification.

The purpose of this document is to ensure consistency, readability, traceability and publication quality across all research outputs.

---

# 2. Scope

These standards apply to every scientific document generated within the NEXUS Research Lab, including but not limited to:

- research manuscripts;
- technical reports;
- conference papers;
- white papers;
- systematic reviews;
- project documentation;
- reviewer reports;
- integrity reports;
- publication packages.

Compliance with these standards SHALL be mandatory unless explicitly overridden by external publication requirements.

---

# 3. Editorial Principles

Every scientific document SHALL adhere to the following principles:

- clarity;
- precision;
- objectivity;
- consistency;
- reproducibility;
- traceability;
- transparency;
- neutrality;
- technical rigor;
- editorial coherence.

Editorial quality SHALL never compromise scientific accuracy.

---

# 4. Scientific Writing Standards

Scientific writing SHALL:

- employ precise technical terminology;
- avoid ambiguous expressions;
- distinguish evidence from interpretation;
- define specialized terminology when appropriate;
- maintain logical argument progression;
- support every scientific claim with appropriate evidence;
- preserve consistency throughout the document.

Scientific writing SHALL NOT:

- include unsupported conclusions;
- exaggerate findings;
- introduce speculative statements as facts;
- omit methodological limitations.

---

# 5. Structural Standards

Every scientific document SHALL follow a clearly defined logical structure.

Unless external publication requirements specify otherwise, documents SHOULD include the following components where applicable:

- Title;
- Abstract;
- Keywords;
- Introduction;
- Background;
- Methodology;
- Results;
- Discussion;
- Conclusions;
- Limitations;
- Future Work;
- References;
- Appendices.

Sections SHALL appear in a logical sequence supporting scientific comprehension.

---

## 5.1 Section Consistency

Each section SHALL:

- serve a single primary purpose;
- avoid redundant information;
- maintain continuity with adjacent sections;
- contribute to the overall research narrative.

Section titles SHALL remain descriptive and technically accurate.

---

## 5.2 Document Hierarchy

Documents SHALL maintain hierarchical organization using consistent heading levels.

The recommended hierarchy is:

- Level 1 — Major Sections
- Level 2 — Subsections
- Level 3 — Detailed Components
- Level 4 — Supporting Elements

Heading hierarchy SHALL NOT skip intermediate levels without justification.

---

# 6. Language Standards

Scientific language SHALL prioritize precision over stylistic complexity.

Documents SHALL:

- use formal academic language;
- avoid colloquial expressions;
- maintain grammatical correctness;
- preserve consistent terminology;
- avoid unnecessary repetition;
- distinguish observations from interpretations.

---

## 6.1 Terminology

Technical terminology SHALL:

- remain consistent throughout the document;
- be defined when first introduced, if necessary;
- follow internationally accepted scientific usage;
- avoid unnecessary synonyms for identical concepts.

The Glossary SHALL be the preferred reference for terminology standardization.

---

## 6.2 Writing Style

Scientific writing SHOULD be:

- concise;
- objective;
- coherent;
- evidence-driven;
- logically structured.

The writing style SHALL avoid:

- emotional language;
- persuasive rhetoric;
- unsupported certainty;
- subjective opinions presented as facts.

---

# 7. Citation Standards

Every scientific statement requiring external support SHALL include an appropriate citation.

Citation practices SHALL:

- acknowledge original authors;
- preserve attribution;
- avoid plagiarism;
- support reproducibility;
- comply with the selected citation style.

Citation style MAY include:

- APA;
- IEEE;
- Vancouver;
- Chicago;
- MLA;

or any journal-specific format approved by the Director.

---

## 7.1 Reference Quality

References SHOULD prioritize:

- peer-reviewed journals;
- systematic reviews;
- meta-analyses;
- official standards;
- governmental publications;
- recognized scientific organizations.

Secondary sources SHOULD only be used when primary literature is unavailable.

---

## 7.2 Citation Integrity

Citations SHALL:

- correspond to actual referenced material;
- accurately support associated claims;
- avoid misleading attribution;
- remain fully traceable.

Fabricated references SHALL be considered a critical integrity violation.

# 8. Figures and Tables

Figures and tables SHALL enhance scientific communication without replacing textual explanation.

Every figure and table SHALL:

- have a unique identifier;
- include a descriptive title;
- provide sufficient contextual information;
- be referenced within the document text;
- maintain numbering consistency.

Figures SHALL prioritize readability over visual complexity.

---

## 8.1 Figure Standards

Scientific figures SHOULD:

- accurately represent underlying data;
- include labeled axes where applicable;
- define measurement units;
- maintain consistent terminology;
- avoid decorative elements that do not contribute to interpretation.

Image manipulation SHALL NOT alter scientific meaning.

---

## 8.2 Table Standards

Tables SHALL:

- organize information logically;
- maintain consistent formatting;
- define abbreviations when necessary;
- avoid duplicated information already presented elsewhere.

Tables SHOULD facilitate comparison rather than narrative explanation.

---

# 9. Version Control

Every scientific document SHALL maintain version traceability.

Version control SHALL include:

- document identifier;
- version number;
- publication status;
- revision history;
- responsible contributor;
- approval status.

Historical versions SHALL remain recoverable.

---

## 9.1 Document Status

Recommended document states include:

- Draft
- Internal Review
- Technical Review
- Scientific Review
- Publication Ready
- Published
- Archived

Each transition SHALL be documented.

---

## 9.2 Change Management

Significant revisions SHOULD document:

- modified sections;
- rationale for changes;
- affected components;
- approval authority;
- revision date.

Minor editorial corrections MAY be grouped into a single revision.

---

# 10. Compliance

A document SHALL be considered compliant with this standard if it:

- follows the prescribed structure;
- applies consistent terminology;
- preserves editorial coherence;
- complies with citation requirements;
- maintains version traceability;
- supports scientific reproducibility;
- satisfies governance requirements.

Non-compliant documents SHOULD be returned for correction before progressing through the workflow.

## Annex B — Editorial Quality Principles

Every document generated within NEXUS SHOULD satisfy the following quality attributes:

- scientific accuracy;
- editorial consistency;
- readability;
- reproducibility;
- traceability;
- neutrality;
- transparency;
- maintainability.

These principles SHALL apply regardless of discipline.

---

## Annex C — Editorial Workflow

Every scientific document SHOULD conceptually follow the editorial lifecycle below.

```text
Planning

↓

Draft Preparation

↓

Technical Review

↓

Scientific Review

↓

Editorial Refinement

↓

Integrity Verification

↓

Publication Preparation

↓

Final Approval

↓

Publication

↓

Archive
```

This lifecycle SHALL remain compatible with the NEXUS Workflow specification.
